0.2.0

2026-11-25

A physics release. The Protostar now lives a whole star’s life in about three minutes: a first collapse, hydrogen burning at 10 to 20 MK, a helium flash, then a carbon core. The Sticky dust becomes a granular medium with coherent supernovae. Every change is checked by unit tests and by acceptance scenarios run on the GPU.

Protostar

  • Gas collisions. Particles collide in cells (multiparticle collision dynamics), so the gas behaves as an ideal gas: isotropic pressure and real shocks.
  • Real fusion laws. The proton-proton chain and the triple alpha follow Gamow’s laws, depend on the local share of fuel, and are corrected for the noise of the measured temperature.
  • Temperature with the molecular weight. At the same velocity dispersion, helium reads 2.65 times hotter than hydrogen, as a thermometer would.
  • Fusion keeps every particle. A particle is a parcel of constant mass that changes type when it fuses: mass and momentum are conserved exactly. The statistics show the share of mass of each element.
  • Radiative cooling of a thin disc (a T⁴ law with the optical depth of each column), with sliders for the cooling rate, the opacity and the minimum temperature.
  • Adaptive thickness: the gas layer is as thick as its own gravity and heat allow, which sets the softening of gravity and the density the fusion sees.
  • Nested grid: a grid 8 times finer is laid over the core once it forms.
  • Smoother gravity with a 3 × 3 (TSC) deposit, and a Jeans floor that keeps the gas from fragmenting at the scale of the grid.
  • Second-order integration with an adaptive time step: wall bounces no longer inject energy, and a gravity-only cloud (optional features off) keeps its energy within 0.2 % over 100 orbits.
  • Initial cloud set by energies: new sliders for the virial ratio and the rotation energy, the true rotation curve of a disc, and options for a Maclaurin density profile and correlated (Larson) turbulence.
  • New statistics: energy drift and virial ratio.
  • Stability map: an overlay of Toomre’s Q and the cooling parameter β of every cell, showing where the disc can fragment.
  • Regrouped options: the Physics tab is split into titled sections (gravity, heating and cooling, collisions), and each switch sits with the sliders it changes: the adaptive thickness under the gravity softening, which is greyed out while it is on, and the Jeans floor with the cooling. The Features tab, now called Advanced, keeps the switches that change how the model is computed or observed.
  • Cooling law: a choice between the radiation (T⁴) and the constant damping of the first Protostar, whose rate does not depend on the temperature, so a cold cloud keeps cooling, falls in and fragments. Each law has its own sliders.
  • Two Protostar configurations: Simple fusion, the default, has the look of the first Protostar with a simpler and lighter physics (every option of the Advanced tab off, no collisions, Jeans floor or adaptive thickness, constant damping for the cooling): livelier and smoother on modest GPUs. Calibrated Protostar turns on every advanced option with the recalibrated values (G 6, radiation cooling 5, λ₀ 6·10⁻³, feedback 1): closer to a real protostar, but much heavier on the GPU. Pick it in the configuration list.

Sticky dust

  • Granular dust: grains resist compression by their agitation, stick faster where the dust is dense and agitated (Haff’s law), and bounce when they meet faster than the new collision speed slider. Particles glow with the agitation around them, not with their speed.
  • Isotropic, faster gravity: the pull of a clump no longer depends on the direction (4 to 5 % instead of about 40 %), and is computed once per grid cell instead of once per particle.
  • Coherent supernovae: they fire on the share of mass in the densest zone, found on the GPU without reading the particles back (fewer stutters); the ejecta fly out in layers, heavy elements slower, all still pulled by gravity; the shock wave slows down as R ∝ t½.

Interface

  • The release number is shown next to the Beta badge and links to these notes (the link moved out of the author overlay).
  • A Features tab lists the optional parts of each algorithm as switches, applied live.
  • New or reworked sliders for both algorithms, each documented on hover in the five languages.

Compatibility and performance

  • The full Protostar model costs more GPU time: the first-visit benchmark sets a lower particle cap on the same device.
  • Configurations saved with 0.1.0 keep their stored values; parameters that did not exist take their default and removed sliders are dropped. Load the built-in Default configuration to get the calibrated model.

For developers

  • A dev-only GPU lab (lab.html, scripts/run-lab.mjs) runs acceptance scenarios on the real simulation in headless Chrome; it is never part of the build.
  • Every law has a pure CPU twin under test (672 tests). The physics is documented in docs/algorithms.md and the design notes in docs/spikes/.

0.1.0

2026-11-19

The first public version.

  • A GPGPU particle simulator in the browser (TypeScript, Three.js, WebGL2), up to 3 million particles.
  • Two algorithms: Sticky dust (multi-scale gravity on a mipmapped density grid, pressure, viscosity, supernovae with a shock wave) and Protostar (softened gravity on 128² grids, cooling, H → He → C fusion whose heat slows the collapse).
  • The first 10 elements with their real atomic masses, sizes and CPK colors; a simple and an advanced element editor; element configurations saved in the browser.
  • Whole-simulation configurations: save, duplicate, rename, load.
  • Static drawings: attractors and repellers drawn with the mouse, imported from an image or written as text.
  • A mouse toolbar: point force (gravity or vortex), brush tips, eraser, shapes.
  • HDR rendering: nebula, gravitational lens, bloom, vignette, grain and tone mapping.
  • Pause, speed, zoom and pan, keyboard shortcuts.
  • Interface in English, French, German, Polish and Spanish.
  • A GPU benchmark on the first visit that sets the particle cap.
